# Product

## Register

brand

## Users

Parents of kids ages 6-15 on the Denver/Front Range, choosing summer activities during the school year. They're evaluating trust, safety, and what their child will experience. Context: scrolling from a phone or laptop at home or work, making a decision by late spring. They want to feel confident before handing over payment; they're not looking for hype, they're looking for competence.

## Product Purpose

Drive summer camp registrations for Wild Child Camp in Westminster, CO. The primary action is clicking through to the FunJoin registration system to browse programs and enroll. Success is measured in registrations, especially filling the most popular weeks before sessions sell out. The site is a single-page marketing experience; it is not a tool or app.

## Brand Personality

Bold, Wild, Trustworthy. Voice: confident and direct, like a Colorado mountain guide who has done this for 26 years. Not hype-y. Not corporate-warm. The brand name itself holds the tension this site must embody: untamed outdoor adventure that parents can trust with their children.

## Anti-references

- Generic camp stock photo sites: cheesy clipart-heavy aesthetics, 2005-era summer camp visuals, bright primary colors on white, sunshine-and-smiley-faces defaults
- Corporate daycare / education brands: sanitized institutional feel, Learning Tree or KinderCare vibes, design that reads "licensed facility" over "real adventure"
- Extreme sports / energy drink brands: too edgy, dark, or aggressive for a parent audience making a safety decision
- SaaS landing page templates: gradient hero, hero-metric grids, identical card rows with icon + heading + text, trust-badge strips

## Design Principles

1. **Earned trust, not asserted trust.** Show the experience and credibility; don't just state it. 26 years in the field speaks louder than a badge that says "Trusted by families."
2. **Wild but responsible.** The brand tension is the message. Adventure and safety are not opposites; the design should hold both without collapsing into either extreme.
3. **Colorado-rooted, not generic-outdoor.** Specificity matters. Westminster, the Front Range, Boulder, the real terrain — these are the details that signal authenticity to local families.
4. **Respect the parent's decision.** Every section earns its place. Nothing decorative-only. Parents have limited time and real stakes; the site should move them forward, not perform for them.
5. **The child's joy is the argument.** Copy is parent-facing, but energy is child-facing. The site should make parents picture their kid's best summer.

## Accessibility & Inclusion

WCAG 2.1 AA. No specific known user needs beyond standard best practices. Ensure sufficient contrast for the ember/tangerine palette against dark backgrounds, and verify interactive elements meet 44px touch target minimums for mobile parents.
